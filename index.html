Whole-body humanoid navigation
Updated Oct 19 · Three-stage method

01 / Research Proposal

Risk-conditioned whole-body navigation

Navigate from A to B using perception, a target pose, and a risk preference.

Research question: can agent-generated trajectories train one navigation policy to choose different routes and motions according to a risk command?

Proposed method. Reinforcement learning (RL) trains policies through simulation. Distillation trains the student using actions from a teacher policy. Project research plan.

02 / Problem Formulation

Risk-conditioned whole-body navigation

Goal pose g = (x, y, z, yaw)

Target position and heading of the robot base in the world frame.

Risk preference β

Sets how strongly the policy accounts for poor outcomes when choosing its motion.

Observations

Depth observations, robot state, and recent observation history.

Student inputs: observations + goal pose g + risk preference β.

SE(2) describes planar position and yaw; z adds height. The same command is used for walking, jumping, crouching, and hand support. β is a preference, not a failure-probability limit.

03 / Problem Formulation: Risk Preference

Risk preference and route selection

For the same start and goal, compare a faster route with greater failure risk and a slower, safer route.

10%: more conservative → 100%: mean return

Conditional value at risk (CVaR) averages the worst β fraction of returns. The example selects the route with the highest CVaR. A 10% tail fraction is not a 10% failure limit.

04 / Stage1 Motion Tracking

Train a shared motion-tracking policy

Tracker inputs

Current robot state and upcoming reference frames. Each reference contains body poses, velocities, and contact states.

Tracker output and training

Output target joint positions. Train with RL to follow motion clips and transition motions from valid starting frames.

The trained tracker becomes the teacher for Stage3. Freeze its parameters during initial student training.

Starting assumption: a predefined motion library covers the required skills. New skills require additional motion data and tracker training.

05 / Stage2 Agentic Skill Trajectory Synthesis

Generate alternative reference trajectories

The agent selects a route, motion clips, start/end frames, and placement in the scene. It uses tracker executions in simulation to test and revise each sequence.

Here, the agent is the offline synthesis process. The graph describes candidate trajectories; it is not part of the deployed student policy.

06 / Stage2 Agentic Skill Trajectory Synthesis: How to Stitch Trajectories

Stitch trajectories using motion matching

1 · Select a compatible entry frame

Search annotated entry windows using foot positions and velocities, root velocity, and the desired future root path.

2 · Smooth the transition

Inertialization applies the difference between the two motions at the switch, then gradually reduces that difference with a damped spring.

Perceptive Humanoid Parkour (PHP) uses motion matching and inertialization. Preserve the main maneuver from takeoff to landing. Test the stitched reference with the tracker to check physical feasibility.

07 / Stage2 Agentic Skill Trajectory Synthesis: Skill Trajectory Library

Build a simulation-tested trajectory library

Store each reference

Scene, valid initial states, goal pose, motion frames, timing, and contact sequence.

Store its measured performance

Goal completion, time, falls, collisions, and returns across repeated trials. Randomize initial states, friction, and disturbances.

Keep several feasible references for the same task. Their outcome distributions support risk-conditioned selection in Stage3.

Retain failed trials when estimating risk. A failed candidate can prompt the agent to revise the route or transition.

08 / Stage3 Teacher-Student Distillation

Train on states visited by the student

Reference selection

For the sampled scene, start, goal, and β, rank compatible references using their measured returns. Use a consistent rule when routes have equal scores.

Closed-loop teacher supervision

At each student state, the teacher reads the selected reference and produces an action target. Compare teacher and student actions, then update the student.

The student controls the simulation. The teacher recomputes its action from the student’s current state at every step.

This is dataset aggregation (DAgger): teacher labels are collected at student-visited states. Stage3 combines this imitation loss with navigation RL.

09 / Stage3 Teacher-Student Distillation: Reference Timing and Supervision

Teacher supervision criteria

Reference timing: advance through the selected trajectory in order. Preserve takeoff, flight, and landing timing.

Supervision limits: determine tracking-error thresholds from teacher recovery tests. Skip imitation when the teacher cannot reliably correct the state.

PHP excludes imitation loss outside its original tracking threshold. Our proposed extension uses failed student states to identify missing approach and recovery examples. PHP, Appendix B.3.

10 / Stage3 Teacher-Student Distillation: Risk-Conditioned Student Policy

Condition the student on the goal and risk

Inputs during training and deployment

The student receives observation history, goal pose, and β. The teacher’s reference trajectory is used for supervision only.

Train the risk response

Sample different β values for the same tasks. Select suitable teacher references and use the same β in the student’s RL objective.

Initial design: concatenate β and the goal with observation features. The student runs without the teacher or reference library. Longer routes may require an explicit map or additional memory.

11 / Stage3 Teacher-Student Distillation: Risk-Sensitive Policy Optimization

Combine imitation with risk-sensitive RL

Imitation objective

Match teacher actions where supervision is valid. Reduce the imitation weight when it prevents successful alternative motions or recovery.

Navigation objective

Reward goal completion and penalize time, falls, and unintended collisions. Use a risk metric such as CVaR to give poor outcomes more weight.

Both the student input and the training objective depend on β. A distributional critic alone does not make the policy respond to risk commands.

Proximal policy optimization (PPO) updates the policy using estimated action advantages. Distributional PPO (DPPO) uses risk-adjusted values to compute those advantages. Schneider et al., Risk-Aware Quadrupedal Locomotion.

12 / Evaluation

Evaluation Metrics

MetricDefinition
Goal success rateFraction of attempts that reach and stabilize at the target pose before timeout.
Failure rateFraction of attempts with a fall or unintended collision. Planned hand support is allowed.
Navigation efficiencyCompletion time and path length for successful attempts; report timeouts separately.
Risk responseChanges in failure rate, lower-tail return, route, and contact choice as β varies.

Evaluate the deployed student over full navigation attempts and multiple training seeds. Report success alongside failure rate so that stopping cannot appear to be successful navigation.

13 / Related Work

Relation to PHP and risk-aware locomotion

Perceptive Humanoid Parkour

Provides motion matching, transition smoothing, and teacher supervision during student rollouts.

Risk-Aware Quadrupedal Locomotion

Provides distributional PPO and a policy conditioned on risk preference.

Proposed extension: agent-generated training trajectories

The agent generates and tests alternative trajectories for navigation tasks. Student failures guide additional trajectory generation. Stage3 trains one perceptive policy across tasks and risk preferences.

The contribution to test is whether agent-guided synthesis improves task coverage and navigation performance at a fixed simulation budget. Generalization remains limited by available skills and observations.

Ablation Studies

Student training
